What the weekend actually feels like

Forget the planning. Here’s the part that matters.

  1. 1

    Friday night, first look up

    Tent up by four. At 5:55 the sun drops into the Window, a notch in the mountains 300 yards from camp. By 7:20 it’s fully dark. You step away from the lantern, wait two minutes, and the Milky Way is just… there. Most people have never seen it. This is roughly what it looks like.

  2. 2

    Saturday, timed to the sun

    Casa Grande turns orange over the tent at sunrise. Lost Mine Trail in the cool morning light — a view into Mexico by half past ten. Santa Elena Canyon at four, when the sun finally reaches the walls. Then Sotol Vista: sunset over the whole west side of the park, and we simply stay put while the stars come out, because it’s one of the best places in Texas to watch them do it.

  3. 3

    Sunday, the sunrise and the river

    One early alarm, worth it: first light through the gap under Balanced Rock. Then down to the Rio Grande — a canyon walk, a 105°F spring in the ruins of an old bathhouse, and at 5:55 the Sierra del Carmen across the water turning pink, then red. You will not want to fly home.

Why the sky here is different

Big Bend has the least light pollution of any national park in the lower 48 — the darkest class of sky on the Bortle scale. On a clear night you can count about 2,000 stars; from a city you might see a hundred. The moon is the only thing that can spoil it, so we picked weekends when it barely shows up.

If we go New Year’s instead

Moon times are approximate for Big Bend. Want to see the exact sky for any night? Stellarium is free and runs in the browser.

Northern lights? Probably not. But.

Big Bend is far too south for the aurora on a normal night. Once every few years a huge solar storm pushes it down to Texas — it happened in May 2024. Nobody can predict one months ahead; the warning comes one to three days out.

So it’s not part of the plan. But in the week before we leave we’ll check NOAA’s aurora forecast, and if a big storm is coming, we point the cameras north. With no moon, we’d have the best seat in Texas.

  • Pack for a 30-degree swing and a long nightShirt-sleeve hiking at 2 pm, insulated jacket and beanie by 7 pm, and fourteen hours between sunset and sunrise. A 15–20°F-rated bag and an insulated pad are the right call for the Basin.
  • WindWinter fronts bring 30–45 mph gusts. Stake the tent properly, keep the tripod low, weight the chairs.
  • Dry air and sunEven at 61°F the desert dehydrates you. A gallon of water per person per day, sunscreen, lip balm.
  • Rain is the least of itDecember averages half an inch at Panther Junction. Flash floods are a July–September problem, not ours.

Four days, timed to the light

Every stop is placed at the hour it looks best: canyons when the sun reaches the floor, overlooks at sunset, one sunrise worth getting up for, and the sky every night from 7:20 pm. For New Year’s, slide every date one week later.

Sunrise 7:45 am · sunset 5:55 pm · full dark 7:20 pm. Drive times from the Basin: Sotol Vista 35 min, Santa Elena 1 h 10, Grapevine Hills 40 min, Rio Grande Village 55 min.

Day 1 · Friday, Dec 24

Dallas to the Basin, and the first sunset

  • 5:30 amOut of Addison in the dark. I-20 west; sunrise somewhere past Weatherford.
  • 11:00 amFuel and lunch in Midland. Last big grocery run.
  • 1:00 pmFort Stockton, then US-385 south. Marathon is the last gas before the park.
  • 2:45 pmPersimmon Gap, then Panther Junction to check the daily report and the Grapevine Hills road condition.
  • 3:30 pmUp the Basin Road. Tent up at 5,400 ft, layers on.
  • 5:15 pmWalk the paved Window View Trail. The sun drops into the notch at 5:55 — the best free show in the park, and we get it every evening we want it.
  • 7:20 pmFully dark. Dinner on the stove, then the first look up from camp. No moon, tonight or any night.

Day 2 · Saturday, Dec 25

Mountains in the morning, canyon in the afternoon, stars from a bluff

  • 7:45 amSunrise hits Casa Grande above the campsite and turns it orange. Coffee in hand for that.
  • 8:30 amLost Mine Trail while the light is low and the lot is empty — 4.8 miles, a view into Mexico by 10:30.
  • 12:00 pmLunch at camp; it is genuinely warm now.
  • 1:30 pmRoss Maxwell Scenic Drive south: Mule Ears overlook, Tuff Canyon, Castolon.
  • 3:45 pmSanta Elena Canyon. By now the sun is far enough round to light the mouth of the canyon; the 1.7-mile trail climbs, then drops to the water between the walls.
  • 5:20 pmBack up to Sotol Vista. Sunset at 5:55 with the whole west side of the park and the canyon notch on the horizon.
  • 7:20 pmWe don’t leave. Sotol Vista is one of the park’s own recommended stargazing spots — 360° of horizon, nothing lit for fifty miles. Main sky session; Orion clears the ridge by 10. Camp by 11.

Day 3 · Sunday, Dec 26

One sunrise, one river, one hot spring

  • 6:20 amUp in the dark. Forty minutes to the Grapevine Hills trailhead (6 miles of dirt road — fine when dry; we checked Friday).
  • 7:15 amWalk a mile up the wash by headlamp. First light at 7:45 comes straight through the gap under Balanced Rock, with the Chisos behind.
  • 10:30 amRio Grande Village. Boquillas Canyon Trail in morning light, then the overlook — the Sierra del Carmen wall across the river.
  • 1:00 pmLunch by the river. Then the hot spring: 105°F water in the ruins of a 1909 bathhouse, Mexico ten metres away.
  • 5:15 pmRio Grande Village Nature Trail for sunset. At 5:55 the Sierra del Carmen goes pink, then red, then grey. This is the east side’s famous moment.
  • 7:20 pmDinner at the picnic area, then the last sky session from the Rio Grande Overlook on the road back — the darkest night of the trip, a 1% moon that never really rises. Camp by 10.

Day 4 · Monday, Dec 27

Home

  • 7:45 amCoffee while Casa Grande lights up one more time. Pack camp.
  • 8:30 amOut through Persimmon Gap. Fuel in Marathon or Fort Stockton.
  • 4:30 pmBack at camp. Window View Trail for sunset — paved, five minutes, stunning.
  • 7:30 pmCamp dinner, then the final session on the darkest night of the trip: a 1% moon that never really rises. If NOAA shows a storm in progress, we look north. Otherwise: everything.

Six places, each at its best hour

  • Sunset through the WindowA V-shaped notch in the Basin rim that frames the sunset. From camp it is a five-minute paved walk. We are there every evening at 5:55.
  • Sunrise at Balanced RockA boulder the size of a bus wedged between two others, a mile up a wash in the Grapevine Hills. At 7:45 am the sun comes through the gap. Trail details.
  • Lost Mine Trail in the morningThe best view per mile in the park: 4.8 miles round trip to a ridge above Juniper Canyon, Mexico on the horizon. Morning light, empty parking lot. Trail details.
  • Santa Elena Canyon in afternoon lightLimestone walls 1,500 feet high with the river between them. Mid-afternoon the sun gets down into the mouth of the canyon and the walls glow. Trail details.
  • Sotol Vista at sunset, then starsA bluff on the scenic drive with the whole west side of the park below it and Santa Elena on the horizon. The park’s own pick for stargazing, so we stay after dark.

Good to know

  • The campsite is the one thing with a deadlineSites open six months ahead on Recreation.gov and Christmas week goes fast. I book the morning they open. Backup: Rio Grande Village, down by the river, warmer and also dark.
  • It gets coldUpper 30s most nights; it has snowed in the Basin. A warm sleeping bag and a pad sort it. Bring a beanie.
  • Clouds happenDecember is one of the clearer months, and three nights is our insurance. One cloudy night still leaves two.
  • No firesWood fires are banned park-wide. We cook on a stove and light camp with a lantern and red headlamps — which is also why the stars look the way they do.
  • Four in the carIt works with a roof box and one bag each. If a fifth person appears, we rent something bigger — not a problem, just a decision.
  • The Balanced Rock roadSix miles of improved dirt. Fine for the HR-V when dry; we ask at Panther Junction on Friday and skip it if it has rained.
  • Bears and javelinasReal, and harmless if food stays in the lockers. It does.

Questions

Which weekend is it?

Whichever the three of us agree on. Christmas is darker; New Year’s means nobody misses Christmas. Hit a button at the bottom.

Do I need to book anything now?

No. Say yes; I’ll grab the campsite the morning it opens. Flights get booked late summer.

How cold is cold?

About 40°F at night, low 60s by day — here’s the park’s own weather page. A good sleeping bag makes it comfortable; a hot-water bottle makes it great.

Will I see the Milky Way?

Yes, with your own eyes, from the campsite, on any clear night. The winter version is softer than the famous summer photos, but it’s unmistakable.

Yes, but it’s Christmas.

It is. That’s why there’s a New Year’s option. If we go Christmas, we’re back the 27th.

Passport? Visa?

Canadian citizens: just a passport. Anything else, check early.

Who else is coming?

Shubham flies in from Phoenix Thursday night, Heet from Seattle Thursday evening. Same car, same campsite, same three nights — four of us, which fills the car.
